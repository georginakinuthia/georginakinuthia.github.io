Bio

I am a Sustainable Urban Designer, Registered Architect, and Scholar at the Norman Foster Institute in Madrid. My focus lies in the spatial data-driven analysis of urban environments, particularly through applied research in spatial vulnerability, transit infrastructure, and participatory design within public policy for sustainable cities. Trained as an architect at the University of Nairobi and registered with BORAQS, I have transitioned from traditional design practice to consulting on modeling complex urban systems and infrastructure equity.

In my current role at the Norman Foster Institute, my work centers on the development of GIS-based spatial vulnerability frameworks in Mexico City to analyze building morphology, transit catchments, and socio-spatial risk distribution. By parsing empirical open datasets (INEGI, CONEVAL, SEMOVI) alongside neighborhood governance networks, I structure multi-scalar decision-support toolkits that direct public policy and funding into targeted resilient interventions. Proficient in tools including Python (GeoPandas), QGIS, Rhino 3D, and ABM, I contribute to solutions integrating urban data science, climate-responsive design, and social equity.

In parallel, I collaborate with international development agencies, municipal governments, and architectural studios across Kenya, Haiti, and Somalia—including the FAO, USAID, and SHOFCO. My applied practice spans decentralized circular infrastructure (such as the 5 tons/day BSF Bioconversion Hub under the FAO Green Cities Initiative), climate-adapted educational facilities, and trauma-informed civic spaces, integrating low-carbon material systems and community advisory protocols into planning matrices.

I maintain academic ties with the University of Nairobi (Kenya) and the Norman Foster Institute (Spain), while actively collaborating with international development networks and municipal planning bodies across Europe and the Global South.
