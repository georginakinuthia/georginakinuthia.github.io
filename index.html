Bio

I am a Sustainable Urban Designer, Registered Architect (BORAQS) and Scholar at the Norman Foster Institute in Madrid. My focus lies in the spatial data-driven analysis of urban environments, particularly through applied research in spatial vulnerability, transit infrastructure, and participatory design within public policy for sustainable cities. Trained as an architect at the University of Nairobi Department of Architecture and licensed with BORAQS, I have transitioned from traditional design practice to consulting on modeling complex urban systems and infrastructure equity.

In my current role at the Norman Foster Institute, my work centers on the co-development of an 8-layer GIS spatial vulnerability framework in Mexico City analyzing building morphology, transit catchments, and socio-spatial risk distribution. By parsing empirical open datasets from INEGI, CONEVAL, and SEMOVI alongside neighborhood governance networks, I structure multi-scalar decision-support toolkits that direct public policy and funding into targeted resilient interventions. Proficient in tools including Python (GeoPandas), QGIS, Rhino 3D, and Agent-Based Modeling (ABM), I contribute to solutions integrating urban data science, climate-responsive design, and social equity.

In parallel, my applied practice across Kenya, Haiti, and Somalia includes designing a 5 tons/day BSF Bioconversion Hub under the FAO Green Cities Initiative, alongside climate-adapted educational facilities and trauma-informed civic spaces in collaboration with international development agencies (FAO Green Cities Project, USAID / Iftiin Peace Hub) and community partners (SHOFCO)—cutting embodied carbon by up to 60% through low-carbon material systems and community advisory protocols.

I maintain academic ties with the University of Nairobi (Kenya) and the Norman Foster Institute (Spain), while actively collaborating with international development networks and municipal planning bodies across Europe and the Global South.
