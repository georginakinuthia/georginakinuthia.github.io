Bio

I am an architect and Master's Scholar on the Sustainable Cities programme at the Norman Foster Institute (NFI) in Madrid. My work operates at the intersection of urban science, climate resilience, and participatory master planning—focusing on spatial data, public space equity, and underserved urban communities.

Trained as an architect, I shifted toward multidisciplinary urban systems after my undergraduate research highlighted how communities without access to formal architectural services are systematically excluded from city-making decisions. This led to nine years of professional practice spanning Kenya, Haiti, Somalia, and the United States. Alongside my postgraduate work in Madrid, I continue to collaborate actively with practice consultants and design teams in Kenya on sustainable infrastructure and spatial research initiatives.

Current NFI Research Focus

At the Norman Foster Institute, I co-developed an eight-layer GIS framework to diagnose spatial, morphological, and socio-economic vulnerability across Mexico City's Miguel Hidalgo borough—evaluating transit catchment gaps, building coverage ratios (BCR), and thermal vulnerability.

My core approach is grounded: buildings and urban master plans are not static, isolated enclosures. They are living systems. For infrastructure to remain maintainable, resource-efficient, and socially safe over its lifecycle, complex spatial datasets must reflect the daily, lived realities of the communities on the ground.
