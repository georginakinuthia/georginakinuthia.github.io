Bio

I am Georgina Kinuthia, a sustainable urban designer and architect specializing in using spatial data and participatory design to support more equitable infrastructure planning in resource-constrained cities. Trained as an architect at the University of Nairobi, I have since practiced across Kenya, Haiti, and Somalia — delivering civic, educational, and humanitarian infrastructure for partners including the FAO, USAID, and SHOFCO.

In my current role as a Scholar at the Norman Foster Institute in Madrid, my focus lies in the development of GIS-based spatial vulnerability frameworks to identify risk distribution channels across Mexico City's Miguel Hidalgo borough. By parsing empirical data alongside neighborhood governance networks, I structure multi-scalar action indices that direct public funding into targeted resilient interventions.

In parallel, I partner with architectural studios and development groups to integrate low-carbon, locally source-derived building systems into planning matrices. I maintain close field ties with structural planning advisory committees to ensure complex spatial information remains community-accessible and baseline maintainable over extended lifecycles.
