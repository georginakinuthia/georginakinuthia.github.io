Bio

I am an architect and Master's candidate on the Sustainable Cities programme at the Norman Foster Institute (ES). My focus lies in participatory master planning and GIS-based spatial analysis, applied to public space, infrastructure equity, and underserved communities.

Trained as an architect, I shifted toward multidisciplinary urban practice after my undergraduate thesis identified a structural gap: communities that cannot access architectural services are typically excluded from formal design processes altogether. This led to nine years of practice across Kenya, Haiti, Somalia, and the U.S., and subsequently to postgraduate study in urban design, where I collaborate with politicians, economists, journalists, and mathematicians to produce more rigorous, cross-disciplinary spatial analysis.

As part of this programme, I co-developed an eight-layer GIS framework for diagnosing spatial and socio-economic vulnerability, applied to Mexico City's Miguel Hidalgo borough.

I maintain professional ties with Adaptiv, U-Design Architects, and Disposition Africa, and collaborate with faculty and fellow students at the Norman Foster Institute.
