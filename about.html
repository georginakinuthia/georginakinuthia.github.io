Biography

I am an Urban Systems Architect and Norman Foster Institute Scholar based in Madrid, specializing in climate-resilient infrastructure, spatial analytics, and participatory urban design. My work bridges quantitative city science—using GIS frameworks and Python data modeling—with qualitative community realism to actively address urban inequality across rapidly growing cities in the Global South and Europe.

Throughout my career, I have led and contributed to sustainable spatial designs, circular economy frameworks, and educational infrastructure networks across Kenya, Haiti, and Somalia. These projects were deployed in direct coordination with international development institutions and humanitarian partners, including the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and USAID.

My current research at the Norman Foster Institute (NFI) centers on building morphology, spatial vulnerability indicators, and public transit accessibility frameworks within Mexico City. I hold a Bachelor of Architecture from the University of Nairobi and am a registered professional with the Board of Registration of Architects and Quantity Surveyors (BORAQS) in Kenya.

Core Philosophy

I believe that buildings and public masterplans shouldn't be designed simply as isolated enclosures, but as deeply integrated systems that actively empower their inhabitants. By anchoring technical spatial data metrics directly inside local governance channels and community planning advisory workflows, we ensure complex infrastructure developments remain baseline maintainable, resource-efficient, and socially safe over long-term lifecycles.
